## DECISION TREE (check in order, STOP at first match)
### 1. EXPLAIN_OTHERS?
The teacher asks another student to EXPLAIN, INTERPRET, or RECONSTRUCT
a peer's idea IN THEIR OWN WORDS.
STRICT CRITERIA:
- The teacher explicitly asks a student to demonstrate UNDERSTANDING of
  what another student said
- Must require INTERPRETATION, not just repetition
- Signals: "put that in your own words", "explain what she meant",
  "what do you think he was trying to say?"
POSITIVE:
- "Who can put what he just said in your own words?"
- "Can you explain what Maria meant by that?"
- "What do you think she was trying to say?"
NEGATIVE (not explain_others):
- "What do you think?" (asking for own opinion → add_on)
- "Do you agree?" (asking for stance → agree_disagree)
- "What did he say?" (asking for repetition → restate)
If EXPLAIN_OTHERS → output ["explain_others"]. STOP.
---
### 2. AGREE_DISAGREE?
The teacher EXPLICITLY asks students to take a STANCE or POSITION on
what another student said, using evaluation/judgment language.
STRICT CRITERIA — must contain EXPLICIT stance language:
- "agree" / "disagree"
- "right" / "wrong" / "correct"
- "same idea" / "different opinion"
- "whose side" / "is he/she right?"
- "do you think that's true?"
- "is that correct?"
POSITIVE:
- "Does anyone agree with him?"
- "Do you agree or disagree with what she said?"
- "Is he right about that?"
- "Do you think that's correct?"
- "Whose side are you on?"
- "Does anyone disagree?"
NEGATIVE (these are add_on, NOT agree_disagree):
- "What do others think?" (no explicit stance language)
- "Does anyone have a different idea?" (inviting contribution, not evaluation)
- "What about you?" (soliciting participation, not stance)
- "Anyone else?" (invitation, not evaluation)
- "Do you have something to add?" (extension, not judgment)
THE TEST: Does the teacher's language REQUIRE students to EVALUATE
(right/wrong/agree/disagree) rather than just CONTRIBUTE?
- If evaluation required → agree_disagree
- If contribution invited → add_on
If AGREE_DISAGREE → output ["agree_disagree"]. STOP.
---
### 3. RESTATE?
The teacher asks another student to REPEAT what a peer said (verbatim
or near-verbatim).
STRICT CRITERIA:
- Asks for REPETITION, not interpretation
- Signals: "What did [name] say?", "Can you repeat that?",
  "Did you hear what he said?"
- Usually occurs when a student spoke too softly
POSITIVE:
- "What did Gabriel say? Did you hear what he said?"
- "Can someone repeat what she just said?"
- "Tell us what he said."
NEGATIVE:
- "Can you put that in your own words?" (interpretation → explain_others)
- "What do you think about what he said?" (reaction → add_on)
If RESTATE → output ["restate"]. STOP.
---
### 4. ADD_ON (DEFAULT)
None of the specific codes above apply. The teacher is inviting other
students to ENGAGE WITH, BUILD ON, EXTEND, or CONTRIBUTE TO the ongoing
discussion that a peer started.
Since trigger = yes and addressee = other_student are ALREADY CONFIRMED,
add_on is the natural default. This includes:
- "Anyone else?" / "Any other ideas?"
- "What do others think?"
- "Who else noticed something?"
- "[Name], what do you think?"
- "How about you?" (to different student)
- "Does anyone want to add to that?"
- "What else?" (directed at class)
- "How else did [topic]...?"
- Calling on a new student to continue the same discussion
- Any question to the class that continues the active discussion thread
- Teacher extends/reframes and then invites others to respond within same topic
- **Teacher provides substantive elaboration or summary of the discussion
  topic for the class, even if no explicit question is posed.** The
  elaboration itself is a move that adds to the collective knowledge and
  keeps the discussion alive. Example: "Yeah, so it shows how different
  homes look in the past..." after a student says "A museum."
→ output ["add_on"].
