You are an expert APT coder. A previous analysis has confirmed:
- This teacher turn IS a follow-up on a student's contribution (trigger = yes)
- The teacher is addressing the SAME STUDENT who just spoke (addressee = same_student)
## YOUR TASK
Assign exactly ONE code from the Type A set:
- **revoice**
- **challenge**
- **press_for_reasoning**
- **say_more** (default)
---
## DECISION TREE (check in order, STOP at first match)
### 1. REVOICE?
The teacher's PRIMARY ACTION in this turn is restating/rephrasing the student's
idea in different or clearer words.
STRICT CRITERIA (ALL must be met):
- The teacher puts the student's idea into new words (not just echoing verbatim)
- This restatement IS the main purpose of the turn (not a lead-in to something else)
- The teacher is checking: "Is this what you meant?"
TEST: Remove the restatement — does the turn lose its main purpose?
- If YES → revoice
- If the turn still has a substantial question beyond the restatement → NOT revoice
POSITIVE examples:
- "So you're saying that social media comparison is the main cause?"
- "In other words, you think the government should have acted sooner?"
- "That's the reason why? They're old." (echoing claim back for confirmation,
  and that IS the whole turn)
NEGATIVE (not revoice):
- "OK so you said X. But why do you think that?" (restatement is a lead-in → press or say_more)
- "Five?" (single-word echo → not substantial enough)
- "Good point. Now..." (acknowledgment → not revoice)
If REVOICE → output ["revoice"]. STOP.
---
### 2. CHALLENGE?
The teacher provides SPECIFIC NEW INFORMATION that CONTRADICTS, COMPLICATES,
or THREATENS the student's claim.
STRICT CRITERIA (ALL must be met):
- The student has made a claim or statement
- The teacher introduces FACTUAL CONTENT (counter-example, counter-evidence,
  contextual information) that puts pressure on that claim
- This is not merely questioning — it's providing ammunition AGAINST the student's position
KEY SIGNALS:
- "Well, remember..." + contradicting fact
- "But what about...?" + counter-example
- "Actually..." + correction
- "But [fact that contradicts student]"
- Providing temporal/contextual information that reframes the student's judgment
POSITIVE examples:
- Student: "All wild animals are dangerous." → Teacher: "But what about a rabbit?"
- Student: "They're old." → Teacher: "They're old? Well, remember, they're from
  50, 60 years ago." (reframes "old" by providing context that challenges the
  student's implied evaluation)
- Student: "The earth is flat." → Teacher: "But we've seen photos from space showing curvature."
NEGATIVE (not challenge):
- "Why do you think that?" (no new info, just asking for reasoning → press)
- "Are you sure?" (no counter-evidence → say_more)
- "Hmm, interesting." (no contradiction → not challenge)
- "Can you think of another possibility?" (invitation, not contradiction → say_more)
If CHALLENGE → output ["challenge"]. STOP.
---
### 3. PRESS_FOR_REASONING?
The teacher demands the student explain WHY or provide EVIDENCE/JUSTIFICATION
for a claim they have already made.
THREE STRICT REQUIREMENTS (ALL must be met):
(a) The student has stated a CLAIM, POSITION, or EVALUATIVE STATEMENT
    (not merely a factual answer or a single word)
(b) The teacher's question EXPLICITLY asks for CAUSE, REASON, EVIDENCE,
    or LOGICAL JUSTIFICATION
(c) The expected answer requires a BECAUSE-RELATIONSHIP or logical argument
KEY SIGNALS (positive):
- "Why do you think that?"
- "What's your evidence?"
- "How do you know?"
- "What makes you say that?"
- "Can you prove it?"
- "What's your reasoning?"
- "Why is that important?" (asking for significance = causal)
- "What difference does X make?" (asking for logical consequence)
- "Why?" (when student has made a clear claim)
NEGATIVE (these are say_more, NOT press):
- "What do you mean?" → clarification
- "Can you give an example?" → illustration
- "Tell me more" → elaboration
- "Like what?" → specifics
- "What else?" → extension
- "What happened next?" → narrative
- "How was it?" → description
- "What should they do?" → proposal (not explaining existing claim)
- "Was there a cover on the roof?" → factual detail
- "Who are the they?" → referent clarification
BOUNDARY CASES:
- Student: "Better lighting." Teacher: "What difference does better lighting
  make?" → YES: asks for logical consequence of student's claim. Press.
- Student: "The government should help." Teacher: "What should they do
  specifically?" → NO: asks for detail/proposal, not WHY. Say_more.
- Student: "It's important." Teacher: "Why is it important?" → YES: asks
  for causal reasoning. Press.
- Student: "It's important." Teacher: "Important for whom?" → NO: asks
  for specification, not reasoning. Say_more.
If ALL THREE requirements are met → output ["press_for_reasoning"]. STOP.
---
### 4. SAY_MORE (DEFAULT)
If none of the above clearly apply, the teacher is asking the same student
to continue, elaborate, clarify, specify, extend, or complete their thought.
This is the CATCH-ALL. Any follow-up to the same student that doesn't meet
the strict criteria above falls here.
Common forms:
- "Tell me more"
- "What do you mean by that?"
- "Can you explain?"
- "Like what?"
- "And then what happened?"
- "Can you give an example?"
- "What else?"
- "Go on..."
- Any factual follow-up question about details of what they said
- "Was there a cover on the roof?" (asks for detail within their topic)
- "Who are the they?" (asks for clarification of referent)
→ output ["say_more"].
