## EXAMPLES:

=== TYPE A EXAMPLES (Teacher ↔ Same Student) ===

### Example 1: Say more (clarification from same student)
Context:
[Turn 21] Taurus: They want to impress people.
>>> [Turn 22] Teacher: Who are the they? <<<
[Turn 23] Taurus: People who have the appearance anxiety.

Output: {"turn_id": 22, "codes": ["say_more"], "reasoning": "Addressing SAME student (Taurus). Asking for clarification (who are 'they'?). No reasoning demanded. → say_more"}

---

### Example 2: Say more (asking for opinion/thoughts from same student)
Context:
[Turn 12] Student: The government built new flats.
>>> [Turn 13] Teacher: What do you think about that? What should the government have done instead? <<<
[Turn 14] Student: Maybe they should have given money directly to the families.

Output: {"turn_id": 13, "codes": ["say_more"], "reasoning": "Addressing SAME student. Asking for opinion and alternative ideas. Student can answer with suggestions — no causal logic required. → say_more, NOT press_for_reasoning"}

---

### Example 3: Press for reasoning (explicit WHY to same student)
Context:
[Turn 9] Jessica: To keep order.
>>> [Turn 10] Teacher: Why is it important that they keep order? <<<
[Turn 11] Jessica: Uh, to prevent chaos.

Output: {"turn_id": 10, "codes": ["press_for_reasoning"], "reasoning": "Addressing SAME student (Jessica). Explicitly asks 'WHY is it important' — demands causal explanation. → press_for_reasoning"}

---

### Example 4: Revoice (teacher rephrases student's idea)
Context:
[Turn 33] Amy: I think people feel bad because they compare themselves on social media.
>>> [Turn 34] Teacher: So you're saying social media comparison is the main cause of the anxiety? <<<
[Turn 35] Amy: Yeah, exactly.

Output: {"turn_id": 34, "codes": ["revoice"], "reasoning": "Teacher rephrases Amy's idea in more concise/clear language to confirm understanding and make it available to class. → revoice"}

---

### Example 5: Challenge (teacher supplies counter-argument to same student)
Context:
[Turn 85] Viola: Negative.
>>> [Turn 86] Teacher: Negative ways! But we're helping those people who have anxiety. Can you think of another feature? <<<
[Turn 87] Viola: Maybe a positive affirmation feature.

Output: {"turn_id": 86, "codes": ["challenge"], "reasoning": "Addressing SAME student (Viola). Teacher supplies specific contradiction: 'we're helping people with anxiety' contradicts 'comparing negatively'. → challenge"}

---

### Example 6: Say more, NOT challenge (asking to specify, no contradiction)
Context:
[Turn 83] Viola: Compare with friends.
>>> [Turn 84] Teacher: Compare with friends in a positive way or in a negative way? <<<
[Turn 85] Viola: Negative.

Output: {"turn_id": 84, "codes": ["say_more"], "reasoning": "Addressing SAME student (Viola). Asking her to specify. Teacher does NOT supply any counter-argument or contradiction. → say_more, NOT challenge"}

---

### Example 7: Say more, NOT press_for_reasoning (no explicit WHY)

=== TYPE B EXAMPLES (Teacher → Other Students) ===

### Example 8: Add on (inviting other students to contribute)
Context:
[Turn 40] Student A: Maybe we can add a breathing exercise feature.
>>> [Turn 41] Teacher: That's interesting. Anyone else? Any other ideas? <<<
[Turn 42] Student B: How about a meditation timer?

Output: {"turn_id": 41, "codes": ["add_on"], "reasoning": "Addressing OTHER students (the class). 'Anyone else?' opens the floor to new contributors with new ideas. → add_on"}

---

### Example 9: Add on (redirecting to a new student)
Context:
[Turn 5] Student A: I think the building looks modern.
[Turn 6] Teacher: Good point.
>>> [Turn 7] Teacher: William, what about you? What do you notice? <<<
[Turn 8] William: I think it's too crowded.

Output: {"turn_id": 7, "codes": ["add_on"], "reasoning": "Addressing a DIFFERENT student (William, not Student A). Discussion is ongoing, teacher invites new contributor. → add_on"}

---

### Example 10: Agree/Disagree (explicit agree/disagree language)
Context:
[Turn 3] William: It is pretty good, right?
>>> [Turn 4] Teacher: Does anyone agree with him? <<<
[Turn 5] Student 1: Yes.

Output: {"turn_id": 4, "codes": ["agree_disagree"], "reasoning": "Addressing OTHER students. Explicitly asks 'agree' — requesting students to take a position on William's idea. → agree_disagree"}

---

### Example 11: Restate (ask student B to repeat student A's words)
Context:
[Turn 71] Gabriel: (speaks softly)
>>> [Turn 72] Teacher: What did Gabriel say? Did you hear what he said? <<<
[Turn 73] Lynn: I didn't hear.

Output: {"turn_id": 72, "codes": ["restate"], "reasoning": "Addressing OTHER student (Lynn). Asking her to repeat what Gabriel said. Purpose: ensure listening. → restate"}

---

### Example 12: Explain others (ask student B to interpret student A's thinking)
Context:
[Turn 14] Student C: I think George is going to the state school and won't see Emily.
>>> [Turn 15] Teacher: Who can rephrase what he said in your own words? <<<
[Turn 16] Student D: George needs to go to university and won't be seeing Emily.

Output: {"turn_id": 15, "codes": ["explain_others"], "reasoning": "Addressing OTHER students. Asks them to interpret/rephrase Student C's idea in their own words. Requires understanding, not just repetition. → explain_others"}

=== TRICKY CONTRAST EXAMPLES ===

### Example 16: Multi-code utterance (revoice + add_on)
Context:
[Turn 1] Ryan: It is about something built after the squatter area was destroyed by fire.
>>> [Turn 2] Teacher: Yeah, it is about something built after the fire. What do you think about that, William? <<<
[Turn 3] William: It is pretty good.

Output: {"turn_id": 2, "codes": ["revoice", "add_on"], "reasoning": "First part: teacher repeats Ryan's words → revoice. Second part: teacher turns to William (different student) for his thoughts → add_on. Two moves in one utterance."}

---

### Example 17: add_on, NOT say_more (teacher turns to class)

### Example 18: press_for_reasoning + add_on (multi-code)

[Turn 9] Jessica: To keep order.
>>> [Turn 10] Teacher: Why is it important that they keep order? No? Anyone want to help? <<<
[Turn 11] Jovin: To prevent chaos.

Output: {"turn_id": 10, "codes": ["press_for_reasoning", "add_on"], "reasoning": "'Why is it important' demands reasoning from Jessica → press_for_reasoning. 'Anyone want to help?' turns to class → add_on. Two moves."}

---

### Example 19: add_on, NOT agree_disagree (no explicit agree/disagree language)
Context:
[Turn 60] Student A: I think homework should be banned.
>>> [Turn 61] Teacher: Okay, what do others think? Does anyone have a different view? <<<
[Turn 62] Student B: I think homework is useful.

Output: {"turn_id": 61, "codes": ["add_on"], "reasoning": "Addressing OTHER students. 'What do others think?' invites new contributions. 'A different view' invites alternative ideas but does NOT use explicit agree/disagree language. → add_on, NOT agree_disagree"}

CONTRAST — if the teacher had said:
>>> "Do you AGREE with her that homework should be banned?" <<<
→ This would be agree_disagree because it explicitly asks for a position using agree/disagree language.

### Example 20: Say more, NOT revoice (partial echo is not revoice)
Context:
[Turn 44] Mia: I think social media is bad for teenagers.
>>> [Turn 45] Teacher: Bad for teenagers? What do you mean by "bad"? <<<
[Turn 46] Mia: Like, it makes them feel they're not good enough.

Output: {"turn_id": 45, "codes": ["say_more"], "reasoning": "Teacher echoes a fragment ('bad for teenagers?') but does NOT rephrase or restate Mia's idea in new/clearer words. The main function is asking Mia to clarify what she means by 'bad'. A brief echo followed by a clarification question → say_more, NOT revoice. Revoice requires meaningful REFORMULATION of the student's idea."}
