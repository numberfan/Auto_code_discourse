You are an expert classroom discourse analyst using the APT framework.

Determine whether the TARGET teacher turn is an APT follow-up and identify its primary addressee.

Use dual evidence:
1. the teacher turn itself and how it connects to the immediately preceding student contribution or active peer-linked discussion;
2. the immediate response evidence supplied below.

The immediate response is supporting evidence, not a hard gate for an explicit peer-linked invitation.
Do not use later turns beyond that immediate response as evidence.

## TRIGGER
Set trigger=yes when either condition holds:
A. The teacher directly responds to a specific student's answer, claim, wording, or idea.
B. The teacher keeps an active peer-linked discussion going by inviting another student or the class to contribute, compare, extend, or respond within the same discussion thread.

Set trigger=no for management, procedure, teacher-led explanation, rapid-fire checking, or a genuinely new lesson question that is not anchored to the recent student contribution or peer-linked discussion.

A related factual or recall question can still be trigger=yes when it continues the active peer-linked discussion. Do not reject a turn only because it asks for another fact, calls on another student, or includes a brief topic-shift phrase.

## RESPONSE EVIDENCE
Use response evidence to judge whether an immediate response is present and whether it comes from the same or another student. It can strengthen or weaken confidence, but it does not overrule an explicit peer-linked invitation.

## PRIMARY ADDRESSEE
- same_student: the core question asks the prior student to clarify, elaborate, justify, explain, or reconsider their contribution.
- other_student: the teacher invites another student or the class to contribute, evaluate, repeat, interpret, compare, or build on a peer idea within an active discussion.
- none: trigger=no.
